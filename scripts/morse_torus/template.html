Morse theory on a torus

Two heights · one induced metric · negative gradient flow

The upright height h₀ = z is Morse, but its gradient flow is not Morse–Smale: two trajectories connect the saddles. Tilting the height to hε = z + tan(26°)y removes those connections and gives a Morse–Smale pair with the same induced metric.

Why the tilt works

For ε = tan(26°), the unstable branches of the upper saddle stay in π < v < 2π and run to the minimum. The stable branches of the lower saddle stay in 0 < v < π and come from the maximum. The two curves are disjoint; no saddle-to-saddle connection remains. On this surface, that is enough for the Morse–Smale condition.

Mathematical model, proof, and drawing conventions

The function and the metric

X(u,v) = ((R + r cos v) cos u, r sin v, (R + r cos v) sin u), with R = 2, r = 0.68 and u,v ∈ ℝ/2πℤ. Write A = R + r cos v.

hε = z + εy = A sin u + εr sin v,
g = A² du² + r² dv²,
u̇ = −cos u / A,   v̇ = (sin v sin u − ε cos v) / r.

Both models use this same induced metric and the flow of −gradg hε. The Morse–Smale condition is a condition on the function–metric pair: Wᵘ(p) and Wˢ(q) must intersect transversely for every pair of critical points. Rotating the camera has no effect on that condition.

Wˢ(p) consists of points converging to p as t → +∞; Wᵘ(p) uses t → −∞. Thus dim Wᵘ(p) = index(p), and dim Wˢ(p) = 2 − index(p). Every manifold includes its own critical point.

Put α = arctan ε. For ε = 0 and ε = tan(26°), the four critical points are nondegenerate:

p₄ = (π/2, α), maximum, index 2; p₃ = (π/2, π + α), upper saddle, index 1; p₂ = (3π/2, π − α), lower saddle, index 1; p₁ = (3π/2, 2π − α), minimum, index 0.

These are (u,v) coordinates modulo 2π. At a critical point the Hessian has zero mixed entry, huu = −A sin u and hvv = −r(cos v sin u + ε sin v). Its two diagonal entries are nonzero and give the indicated indices. The critical heights are R + r√(1 + ε²), R − r√(1 + ε²), −R + r√(1 + ε²), and −R − r√(1 + ε²), in that order for the two values of ε shown.

Why the upright height fails

For ε = 0, the inner equator v = π is invariant. Its two open arcs between p₃ and p₂ are trajectories from p₃ to p₂. In fact Wᵘ(p₃) = {v = π} ∖ {p₂} and Wˢ(p₂) = {v = π} ∖ {p₃}. Along either connecting arc their one-dimensional tangent spaces coincide. Their sum is a line, not the two-dimensional tangent plane of the torus, so this pair is not Morse–Smale.

Why the tilted height satisfies the condition

For ε = tan(26°), v̇ = ε/r > 0 at v = π and v̇ = −ε/r < 0 at v = 2π. Thus the closed band π ≤ v ≤ 2π is forward invariant, with the vector field pointing strictly inward at its boundary. The upper saddle p₃ lies in its interior. Its unstable branches remain in this band, whereas the lower saddle p₂ lies in 0 < v < π. Therefore no trajectory can connect p₃ to p₂.

Likewise, 0 ≤ v ≤ π is invariant for the reverse flow and contains the lower saddle p₂. The stable branches of p₂, followed backward, stay in this band. Along every nonconstant forward trajectory, dhε/dt = −‖gradg hε‖² < 0. Compactness and the finite set of critical points imply that each branch has a critical point as its limiting endpoint. The critical heights and the two invariant bands force the unstable branches of p₃ to end at p₁, and the stable branches of p₂ to begin at p₄.

There are only two saddles. Strict decrease of hε rules out saddle homoclinic trajectories and a connection from p₂ to the higher saddle p₃; the band argument rules out the remaining direction. At each saddle its stable and unstable tangent lines are the complementary eigenspaces of the linearized gradient flow. All other nonempty intersections either involve an open two-dimensional manifold or are the transverse stable/unstable intersection at a critical point. Hence every stable/unstable intersection is transverse: (hε, g) is Morse–Smale.

What the picture shows

In the tilted view, the displayed torus is rigidly rotated by α = 26° using (x,y,z) ↦ (x, (y − εz)/√(1 + ε²), (z + εy)/√(1 + ε²)). Displayed vertical height is therefore hε/√(1 + ε²). This rotation preserves the induced metric, and the positive constant factor only rescales flow time.

In the four-manifold view, dark green is Wᵘ(p₃), light green is Wˢ(p₃), magenta is Wˢ(p₂), and violet is Wᵘ(p₂). Arrows follow negative gradient flow. In the single-manifold and comparison views, red denotes unstable and blue denotes stable. In comparison mode, dashed blue is drawn over red wherever the upright curves coincide. The thin multicolored curves are sampled flow trajectories: their individual colors distinguish paths and do not specify a stable or unstable manifold. Dark boundaries in a two-dimensional view are excluded from the highlighted open cell. For curves and points, the pale torus is a reference surface.

The upright manifolds have the exact descriptions given in the readout. Tilted separatrices, open-cell boundaries, and the thin flow lines are sampled numerically; the proof above is independent of that sampling. Endpoints and excluded boundaries have small visual gaps, and lines are lifted slightly off the surface for visibility. These drawing offsets have no mathematical meaning. Highlighted manifolds include their own critical points and exclude other limiting critical points. The see-through view reveals curves on the back; transparency can introduce rendering artifacts.
