Wᵘ(p₄)

Dimension 2 · surface

An open two-dimensional cell on the torus. Every point in this region converges to p₄ as time tends to −∞.

The inner equator and the lower meridian are excluded. The gaps are widened slightly to make these exclusions visible.

Mathematical model and conventions

X(u,v) = ((R + r cos v) cos u, r sin v, (R + r cos v) sin u), with R = 2, r = 0.68 and u,v ∈ ℝ/2πℤ.

f = z,   g = (R + r cos v)² du² + r² dv².
Negative gradient flow: u̇ = −cos u / (R + r cos v),   v̇ = sin v sin u / r.

Wˢ(p) consists of points converging to p as t → +∞; Wᵘ(p) uses t → −∞. Thus dim Wᵘ(p) = index(p), and dim Wˢ(p) = 2 − index(p). Every manifold includes its own critical point.

The inner equator is v = π. The upper meridian is u = π/2, and the lower meridian is u = 3π/2. The dark curves shown in a 2D view are excluded from the highlighted open cell. For curves and points, the pale torus is only a reference surface. The optional see-through view reveals curves on the back; transparency can introduce rendering artifacts.

This pair is not Morse–Smale. The two inner-equator trajectories connect p₃ to p₂. Along them Wᵘ(p₃) and Wˢ(p₂) coincide and do not meet transversely. A Morse function alone does not imply a Morse–Smale gradient flow.

Curve endpoints and excluded curves are drawn with small gaps for visibility. Lines are lifted slightly off the surface so they remain visible. Highlighted geometry represents the manifold itself, not its closure. The thin flow lines are numerical samples; the stable and unstable manifolds use the exact descriptions above.
