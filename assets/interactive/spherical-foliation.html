Why this is a singular foliation

The leaves

f(x) = ½‖x‖²,
Lc = f⁻¹(c) = {x : ‖x‖ = √(2c)} for c > 0,
L₀ = {0};   f⁻¹(c) = ∅ for c < 0.

Every positive level is a connected two-dimensional sphere. Together with the origin these leaves partition ℝ³, but their dimensions are not constant: the origin is a zero-dimensional singular leaf.

Tangent directions from rotations

The rotation vector fields preserve f:

X₁₂ = x₁∂₂ − x₂∂₁,
X₁₃ = x₁∂₃ − x₃∂₁,
X₂₃ = x₂∂₃ − x₃∂₂.

At x ≠ 0, their span is x⊥ = ker(dfx), the two-dimensional tangent plane to the sphere. Their flows are rotations, whose orbits are exactly these spheres and the origin. Their smooth-function span is closed under Lie brackets and gives a singular foliation.

All three fields vanish at 0, so the tangent space of its leaf is {0}. Although df₀ = 0 and therefore ker(df₀) = ℝ³, that kernel is not the tangent space of the singular leaf. Taking the differential's kernel point by point does not give the leaf tangent distribution at the critical point.

Gradient paths cross the leaves

For the Euclidean metric, grad f(x) = x. The negative gradient equation and its solution are

ẋ = −x,   x(t) = e−tx(0),
f(x(t)) = e−2t f(x(0)).

The optional dashed radial paths cross spherical leaves toward the origin; they are not leaves of this foliation. A nonzero trajectory approaches the origin only as t → +∞. Arrows point in the direction of negative gradient flow. The breathing animation instead varies the selected value of c back and forth, without moving a point along a gradient trajectory.

Drawing conventions

The nested view samples three other levels, c = 0.16, 0.49, and 1.69; the foliation contains a sphere for every c > 0. The cutaway omits a quarter of each rendered sphere to reveal the inner leaves and origin. This is only a drawing choice: the actual leaves have no holes or boundaries. Transparency can produce overlap artifacts. World coordinates use the same scale in all three directions.

The spheres and the origin are the orbits of SO(3). For singular foliations generated by group actions, see Singular foliations notes, §1.2.3.
