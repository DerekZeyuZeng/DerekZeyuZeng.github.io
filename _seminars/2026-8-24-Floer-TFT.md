This page serves as the homepage for our upcoming informal fall 2026 reading seminar on bordism and Floer field theories. The main theme is to explore how ideas from bordism and topological field theory connect to the geometric and algebraic structures that appear in Floer theory.

The seminar will run from August 24 to December 10, 2026, and will be organized as a slow, discussion-driven reading cycle. We will work through definitions, examples, and key constructions together, with an emphasis on understanding the geometric and topological meaning behind the algebraic machinery.

The current plan is to begin with the basic language of bordism and cobordism, then move toward the functorial viewpoint of topological field theories, and finally connect these ideas to Floer-theoretic and related geometric constructions.

## Tentative Weekly Schedule

The schedule is organized around a single guiding theme:

> How does geometric cutting and gluing give rise to functorial, Floer-theoretic, and homotopical structures?

Each meeting will focus on a small collection of definitions, one principal theorem or construction, and at least one concrete example. The schedule may be adjusted according to the backgrounds and interests of the participants.

### Week 1 — Classical Bordism and the Pontryagin–Thom Construction

- **Topics:** Bordisms and bordism groups; oriented and framed bordism; normal framings; the Pontryagin–Thom collapse map.
- **Main objective:** Understand how a geometric bordism problem is translated into a homotopy-theoretic problem.
- **Reading:**
  - [Dan Freed, *Bordism: Old and New*, Lectures 1–3](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/index.html)
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 2: Orientations, Framings, and the Pontryagin–Thom Construction](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture2.pdf)
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 3: The Pontryagin–Thom Theorem](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture3.pdf)
- **Guiding question:** Why should framed bordism groups be stable homotopy groups of spheres?

### Week 2 — Tangential Structures, Thom Spectra, and Bordism Categories

- **Topics:** Stable tangential structures; orientation, spin, and framing structures; Thom spaces and Thom spectra; bordisms as morphisms.
- **Main objective:** Pass from individual bordism groups to the spectrum-level and categorical organization of bordisms.
- **Reading:**
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 9: Tangential Structures](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture9.pdf)
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 10: Thom Spectra and \(X\)-Bordism](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture10.pdf)
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 14: Bordism Categories](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture14.pdf)
- **Guiding question:** What additional information is retained when bordisms are organized into a category rather than collapsed to equivalence classes?

### Week 3 — The Atiyah–Segal Axioms for Topological Field Theory

- **Topics:** Symmetric monoidal categories; the bordism category; functoriality under gluing; disjoint union and tensor product; orientation reversal and duality.
- **Main objective:** Formulate a \(d\)-dimensional TQFT as a symmetric monoidal functor
  \[
  Z\colon \operatorname{Bord}_d^\theta \longrightarrow \mathcal C.
  \]
- **Reading:**
  - [Michael Atiyah, *Topological Quantum Field Theories*](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1007/BF02698547.pdf)
  - [Dan Freed, *Lectures on Topological Quantum Field Theory*](https://web.ma.utexas.edu/users/dafr/OldTQFTLectures.pdf)
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 14: Bordism Categories](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture14.pdf)
- **Guiding question:** Which geometric gluing operations become algebraic composition laws under a TQFT?

### Week 4 — Duality, Finiteness, and One-Dimensional TQFTs

- **Topics:** Dualizable objects; evaluation and coevaluation bordisms; finite-dimensionality; classification of oriented one-dimensional TQFTs; invertible TQFTs.
- **Main objective:** Derive algebraic finiteness conditions directly from the geometry of bordisms.
- **Reading:**
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 15: Duality](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture15.pdf)
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 16: One-Dimensional TQFTs](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture16.pdf)
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 17: Invertible Topological Quantum Field Theories](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture17.pdf)
  - [Jacob Lurie, *On the Classification of Topological Field Theories*, §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.0465)
- **Guiding question:** Why does the existence of cap and cup bordisms force \(Z(\mathrm{pt})\) to be dualizable?

### Week 5 — Morse–Cerf Theory and Two-Dimensional TQFTs

- **Topics:** Morse functions on bordisms; handle decompositions; elementary bordisms; Cerf moves; commutative Frobenius algebras.
- **Main objective:** Understand how a field theory can be constructed from elementary bordisms and relations among their decompositions.
- **Reading:**
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 23: An Application of Morse–Cerf Theory](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture23.pdf)
  - [Joachim Kock, *Frobenius Algebras and 2D Topological Quantum Field Theories*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511615443)
- **Guiding question:** How do birth–death moves and crossings of critical values become the algebraic relations of a Frobenius algebra?
- **Remark:** The classification of two-dimensional TQFTs is treated as a finite-dimensional model of decomposition independence, rather than as the final destination of the seminar.

### Week 6 — Cobordism Categories and Stable Homotopy Theory

- **Topics:** Topological cobordism categories; classifying spaces of categories; infinite loop spaces; Madsen–Tillmann spectra; the GMTW theorem.
- **Main objective:** Understand the statement and geometric meaning of
  \[
  B\operatorname{Cob}_d^\theta \simeq \Omega^{\infty-1}MT\theta.
  \]
- **Reading:**
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 20: Topological Bordism Categories](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture20.pdf)
  - [Dan Freed, Lectures 21–22: Sheaves on Manifolds and the GMTW Proof Architecture](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/index.html)
  - [Galatius–Madsen–Tillmann–Weiss, *The Homotopy Type of the Cobordism Category*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0605249)
- **Guiding question:** In what sense does a Thom spectrum encode not only bordism groups but the entire homotopy type of a bordism category?
- **Reading level:** The statement and construction should be understood precisely; the complete proof of the GMTW theorem is not required.

### Week 7 — Extended TQFTs and the Cobordism Hypothesis

- **Topics:** Extended locality; higher bordism categories; fully dualizable objects; framed and oriented field theories; the Cobordism Hypothesis.
- **Main objective:** Understand the classification principle that a fully extended framed TQFT is determined by its value on a point.
- **Reading:**
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 24: The Cobordism Hypothesis](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture24.pdf)
  - [Daniel Freed, *The Cobordism Hypothesis*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.5100)
  - [Jacob Lurie, *On the Classification of Topological Field Theories*, §§1–2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.0465)
- **Guiding question:** Why does extending a field theory to lower-dimensional strata strengthen ordinary functoriality into a classification by dualizability?
- **Reading level:** Focus on definitions, examples, and the statement of the Cobordism Hypothesis rather than its complete higher-categorical proof.

### Week 8 — Floer Field Philosophy and Cerf Decompositions

- **Topics:** Categories with Cerf decompositions; the symplectic category; generalized Lagrangian correspondences; the construction principle for Floer field theories.
- **Main objective:** Understand the proposed factorization
  \[
  \operatorname{Bord}_{2+1}
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Symp}
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Cat}.
  \]
- **Reading:**
  - [Katrin Wehrheim, *Floer Field Philosophy*, §§2.1–2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.04908)
  - [Dan Freed, Lecture 23: An Application of Morse–Cerf Theory](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~dafr/M392C-2012/Notes/lecture23.pdf)
- **Guiding question:** What additional analytic and categorical data are needed to replace the algebraic assignments of an ordinary TQFT by Floer-theoretic assignments?

### Week 9 — Lagrangian Correspondences and Gauge-Floer Theory

- **Topics:** Lagrangian correspondences; geometric composition; quilted Floer cohomology; moduli spaces of flat connections; the Atiyah–Floer conjecture.
- **Main objective:** Study the field-theoretic assignments
  \[
  \Sigma\longmapsto \mathcal M_{\mathrm{flat}}(\Sigma),
  \qquad
  Y\colon\Sigma_0\to\Sigma_1
  \longmapsto
  L_Y\subset
  \mathcal M_{\mathrm{flat}}(\Sigma_0)^{-}
  \times
  \mathcal M_{\mathrm{flat}}(\Sigma_1).
  \]
- **Reading:**
  - [Katrin Wehrheim, *Floer Field Philosophy*, §§2.5–2.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.04908)
  - [Wehrheim–Woodward, *Functoriality for Lagrangian Correspondences in Floer Theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.2851)
  - [Michael Atiyah, *New Invariants of 3- and 4-Dimensional Manifolds*](https://math.berkeley.edu/~gbeiner/texts/Atiyah_Invariants.pdf)
- **Guiding question:** Under what hypotheses does geometric composition of correspondences agree with Floer-theoretic composition?
- **Foundations issue:** The seminar will distinguish established embedded-composition results from conjectural or incomplete general field-theoretic extensions.

### Week 10 — Symplectic Field Theory and Cobordism Maps

- **Topics:** Contact manifolds and Reeb orbits; symplectizations; punctured pseudoholomorphic curves; holomorphic buildings; neck stretching; symplectic cobordism maps.
- **Main objective:** Compare Floer field theory, where symplectic geometry appears in the target, with SFT, where contact manifolds and symplectic cobordisms form the geometric source.
- **Reading:**
  - [Hind–Siegel, *Symplectic Field Theory: An Overview*, selected §§1, 3, and 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19936)
  - [Eliashberg–Givental–Hofer, *Introduction to Symplectic Field Theory*, selected sections](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0010059)
  - [John Pardon, *Contact Homology and Virtual Fundamental Cycles*, Introduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.03873)
- **Guiding question:** How does breaking of holomorphic curves encode algebraic composition and the relation \(\partial^2=0\)?
- **Foundations issue:** Rigorous contact homology results will be distinguished from the more general, partly programmatic full SFT package.

### Week 11 — Floer Homotopy Types and Derived Moduli Spaces

- **Topics:** Flow categories; framed moduli spaces; Pontryagin–Thom realization; spectrum-valued Floer invariants; derived zero loci and Fredholm deformation complexes.
- **Main objective:** Understand how retaining the topology and bordism classes of compactified moduli spaces refines a Floer chain complex to a spectrum.
- **Reading:**
  - [Ralph Cohen, *Floer Homotopy Theory, Revisited*, §§1.1–1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08694)
  - [Abouzaid–Blumberg, *Foundation of Floer Homotopy Theory I: Flow Categories*, Introduction and selected sections](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03193)
  - [John Pardon, *Representability in Non-linear Elliptic Fredholm Analysis*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00184)
- **Guiding question:** What information is retained by a flow category or Floer spectrum that is lost after passing to a chain complex?
- **Derived viewpoint:** For a Fredholm equation \(s(x)=0\), relate its infinitesimal deformation theory to the tangent complex
  \[
  \bigl[T_x\mathcal B\xrightarrow{D_xs}\mathcal E_x\bigr].
  \]

### Week 12 — Frontier Synthesis: Toward Spectral Floer Field Theories

- **Topics:** Contact flow categories; exact symplectic cobordisms as flow bimodules; composition and coherence; comparison of ordinary, Floer-theoretic, spectral, and derived field-theoretic structures.
- **Main objective:** Assemble the different levels of structure studied during the seminar:
  \[
  \text{bordisms}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{Lagrangian correspondences or moduli spaces}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{Floer complexes}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{flow categories and spectra}.
  \]
- **Reading:**
  - [Chanda–Hirschi, *A Contact Homotopy Type*, Introduction and main theorems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01821)
  - [Abouzaid–Blumberg, *Foundation of Floer Homotopy Theory I*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03193)
  - [Katrin Wehrheim, *Floer Field Philosophy*, concluding sections](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.04908)
- **Guiding questions:**
  1. What are the source and target categories in each proposed field theory?
  2. Which gluing and composition statements have been proved?
  3. Which constructions remain partial, conditional, or conjectural?
  4. What role might derived differential geometry play in organizing singular or nontransverse moduli spaces?
- **Format:** Short participant presentations followed by a joint comparison and foundations discussion. The goal is to identify the current mathematical landscape rather than to require an original research result.
